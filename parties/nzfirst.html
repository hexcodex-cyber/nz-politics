New Zealand First

Government (coalition partner)

In brief

History
Founded by Winston Peters in 1993 after he left National. It has been in government with National (1996–98, 2023–) and Labour (2005–08 confidence and supply; 2017–20 coalition). It was out of Parliament in 2008–11 and 2020–23, and returned in 2023 with 8 seats.
Ideology / positioning
Nationalist and populist. Socially conservative and economically interventionist in places ('common sense'). Opposes what it calls race-based policy and 'woke' policy.
Current role
Coalition partner in government. Peters is Minister of Foreign Affairs (and was Deputy PM until May 2025). Shane Jones holds Resources, Regional Development and Oceans and Fisheries.

Key 2026 election policies

  • 'Commonsense Express' 10-point rail plan (27 October 2026, Peters at a public meeting in Levin): nearly $4b in total, much of it already allocated or underway, including the Rail Network Investment Programme, the Marsden Point Rail Link, new Cook Strait ferries in 2029, making Te Huia permanent ($130m for new trains), permanent Christchurch passenger rail, level-crossing removals ($300m), Kaimai Tunnel capacity, Auckland and Wellington rail companies co-owned by taxpayers and ratepayers, and planning for Auckland's third and fourth main lines. [RNZ]
  • Fuel and the ETS (24 October 2026, Peters at a public meeting in Thames): remove the Emissions Trading Scheme charge from fuel over the next term, which NZ First says would cut about 12 cents a litre off petrol and 14 cents off diesel; it says the ETS took $850m from fuel consumers and businesses in the past year. [nzfirst.nz]
  • Accelerated depreciation (23 October 2026, Peters at Pact Packaging, East Tāmaki): a 50% first-year depreciation rate for new plant, machinery and equipment in manufacturing and targeted export sectors (mining, aquaculture, forestry, farming, fishing), on top of the existing 20% Investment Boost, to be implemented at the next Budget. Peters put the cost at $2.4b in 2026/27 and $2.5b in 2027/28. [interest.co.nz]
  • Law and order (22 October 2026, Peters in West Auckland): a degrees-of-murder regime raising the minimum non-parole period from 10 to 20 years, life without parole for first-degree murder and for the rape or murder of children, an eight-year minimum non-parole period for fatal 'coward punches', investigating 'stand your ground' self-defence laws, and replacing the Serious Fraud Office with a Police division plus a dedicated organised crime agency. [RNZ]
  • Defence (reported by the NZ Herald on 21 October 2026; written by former defence minister Ron Mark): defence spending of 3% of GDP by 2032, merging the Army, Navy and Air Force headquarters into a Joint Defence Command, a Marine Force of up to 1200, 12,500 regular personnel, 6000 reservists and 4000 civilians, a 90-day recruitment pipeline, a cap on senior executive pay and 'strictly merit' appointments. Mark could not say whether it would survive coalition talks. [NZ Herald]
  • Video games (20 October 2026): raise the game development rebate from 20% to 40% of costs, lift the cap per studio from $3m to $10m a year and lower the minimum spend from $250,000 to $150,000, with the total budget rising from $40m to $45m by 2028; guarantee Dunedin's Centre of Digital Excellence $5m a year rising to $7m. Labour has promised a smaller expansion (25%, capped at $4.5m). [RNZ]
  • A referendum on capping resident visas at 20,000 a year, and 'substantially' reducing non-essential immigration. [RNZ]
  • Abolish the Waitangi Tribunal within 100 days of the next government. [X]
  • Buy back BNZ (Luxon: 'absolute madness'). Split the big power companies (secondary source). [RNZ]
  • Repeal the Regulatory Standards Act. Give ministers the final say on fast-track approvals. [RNZ]
  • Replace the Accredited Employer Work Visa with a 'shortage-led' Skilled Work Visa tied to occupation, region and 'demonstrated need' (announced 11 October in Dunedin). [RNZ]
  • Fast-track residential approvals (14 October): developers must show the roads, pipes and other infrastructure a fast-tracked housing project needs exists, is funded and can be delivered; dedicated housing expert panels could decline projects; the housing minister would make referral decisions. Luxon called it 'quite bizarre' and Bishop said it is already in the law. (Corrected from 13 October: Peters' X post went up at 8:03am NZDT on 14 October.) [RNZ]
  • Coastguard (15 October 2026): raise the Crown's share of Coastguard funding from 40% to 70% (about $12m a year more) and review how emergency services coordinate. [RNZ]
  • Combat sports (15 October 2026): a statutory Combat Sports Commission to license events and register contestants, minimum medical standards and mandatory stand-down periods, and penalties for unsanctioned events. [RNZ]
  • Canterbury rail (18 October 2026): a 12-month commuter rail trial in Canterbury (aiming for Rolleston to central Christchurch in about 20 minutes), working with the operators of the Mainlander service, plus bringing back the Southerner train between Christchurch, Dunedin and Invercargill. Peters said a permanent service would depend on passenger numbers and economic sense. [RNZ]
  • Cash (19 October 2026, Peters on X): a law requiring every company, bank, organisation and government office to accept cash for payments and transactions. Peters says the legislation is written and would be introduced immediately after the election. [X]
  • A Fonterra 'Use It or Lose It' milk-access policy. Retirement village reform. [Farmers Weekly]
  • Aluminium (16 October 2026): after visiting Vulcan Aluminium in Hamilton, Peters said NZ First would reverse 'before the end of this year' the Consumer Affairs Minister's decision this week not to impose a safeguard measure on imported aluminium extrusions, saying thousands of jobs and all nine local extrusion pressers were at risk. [X]

Notable 2025–26 events and controversies

  • 29 October 2026: at TVNZ's Kaupapa Māori Debate Shane Jones defended abolishing the Waitangi Tribunal, said 'We're going to mine', and committed to introducing a bill to force a Ngāpuhi Treaty settlement within the first 100 days of a new government; Labour's Willie Jackson called it 'absolute nonsense'. In an interview with Whakaata Māori's Mata with Mihingarangi (reported by RNZ on 30 Oct) Peters called his Hutt South endorsement of Chris Bishop a 'statement of respect', not a deal, and said the Treaty had become the 'trough of Waitangi'. [RNZ]
  • 28 October 2026: at a Lower Hutt meeting Peters endorsed National's Chris Bishop in Hutt South (his second cross-party endorsement as leader, after Labour's Kelvin Davis in 2014), called this 'the most sickening election', said 'there's no way those polls are accurate', called Luxon and Hipkins 'tweedle dum and tweedle dee', and said 'No more third-world people coming in for third-world wages'. A man giving Nazi salutes was escorted out ('naff off', Peters said). Earlier, replying on X to Qiulae Wong, Peters wrote that NZ First had 'long ago ruled out working with this bunch of pinko communists'. At Whakaata Māori's Māori leaders' debate the same night Shane Jones clashed with Labour's Willie Jackson. [RNZ]
  • 27 October 2026: a social media ad by NZ First candidate and former defence minister Ron Mark blamed the 2024 sinking of HMNZS Manawanui on 'woke b******t of diversity, equity and inclusiveness'. Mark denied it was about the ship being captained by a woman, but Peters said it was 'clear as daylight' the captain was hired through a DEI programme and that he had evidence. At a Levin meeting of about 300 people Peters warned National voters against 'wasting' their party vote, repeated that NZ First wants out of the Paris Agreement, called Opportunity the 'Opportunist' Party and said he would not go into government with 'pinko communists'. He dismissed NZ First's two-point rise in the 1News–Verian poll: 'We don't believe in these polls.' [RNZ]
  • 23 October 2026: Peters said Leoni's resignation underscored Labour's 'incompetence' but that NZ First would not be 'salivating over somebody else's misery'. [RNZ]
  • 22 October 2026: Peters said he respected the 18 Pacific Islands Forum countries' decision to make Israel a partner and was 'horrified' by those criticising it. At the Epsom debate the same night, Green MP Lawrence Xu-Nan denied Peters' claim that the two had spoken directly about Peters' comments to him in the House. [RNZ]
  • 21 October 2026, Powerbrokers' debate: Peters said he had 'thoroughly deserved' the smackings he got as a child, ruled out working with Labour even under Kieran McAnulty or Barbara Edmonds, said Luxon calling him a 'numpty or a yahoo' was 'just inexperience', called Luxon's $30b estimate for buying back BNZ 'rubbish', and called himself a nationalist against 'globalists'. [NZ Herald]
  • 21–22 October 2026: after the High Court set aside the government's restrictions on new puberty-blocker prescriptions, NZ First said it would demand the Health Minister appeal immediately; Peters said the government might need to legislate. [Scoop]
  • 20 October 2026: Peters called the 1News–Verian poll (NZ First down two to 8%) 'crap' and said NZ First would 'do something about the TV1 polls' after the election. Asked if that was a threat, he said it was not. The same day he said he does not feel safe as an MP after the attack on Australian MP Helen Haines, repeated that Hipkins should have been 'behind bars' over the Covid vaccine rollout, and claimed Labour had approached his staff about coalition options, which Hipkins denied. [1News]
  • July 2026: Peters told Green MP Lawrence Xu-Nan in Parliament 'Go back to your own country'. China's embassy lodged representations with MFAT, Luxon criticised the remark, and Peters refused to apologise. [1News]
  • Napier candidate Stuart Nash resigned after sexist comments, according to 1News. [1News]
  • NZ First voted for the Regulatory Standards Act in November 2025, then pledged to repeal it. [RNZ]
  • Shane Jones became deputy leader on 17 September 2025. Alfred Ngaro (NewZeal) aligned with NZ First in March 2026. [Wikipedia]
  • NZ First used its 'agree to disagree' clause against the NZ–India free trade agreement (secondary source). [Wikipedia]
  • 13 October 2026: Winston Peters took part in TVNZ's Multi-Party Debate, clashing with Waititi over te reo Māori and with Wong over diversity and experience, and predicting NZ First would win a coalition argument on lifting the minimum wage with inflation. [1News]
  • 13–14 October 2026: New Zealand was one of only two countries to abstain on a non-binding UN declaration on pandemic preparedness. Peters made the call as Foreign Minister and said signing would have been 'problematic'; National ministers said they would have voted in favour while registering reservations. [RNZ]
  • 14 October 2026, ASB finance debate: Shane Jones said he would not join a cross-party working group 'designed to move criteria or move the age of super', while the other five finance spokespeople said yes or were open to it. [RNZ]
  • 16 October 2026: the Migrant Workers Association called NZ First's plan to untie work visas from a single employer 'a good first step' but criticised past remarks by Peters and Jones. An immigration adviser warned against dismantling the current visa framework. NZ First did not respond to RNZ's requests for comment. [RNZ]
  • 17 October 2026: Peters said NZ First would not release a separate fiscal plan, calling Budget 2026 its 'collective plan' and saying its policies have been costed. At a public meeting in Botany he criticised both the left bloc and his coalition partners on economic growth, took a swipe at ACT's minimum-wage freeze, and praised Education Minister Erica Stanford. [RNZ]
