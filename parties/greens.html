Green Party of Aotearoa New Zealand

Opposition

In brief

History
Formed in 1990, with roots in the Values Party (1972). It was part of the Alliance until 1997 and has had MPs in its own right since 1999. It was in a cooperation agreement with Labour from 2017 to 2023. Chlöe Swarbrick became female co-leader in March 2024, alongside Marama Davidson.
Ideology / positioning
Left. Environmentalist, with a focus on social and economic justice and Te Tiriti.
Current role
Opposition. The Greens have said 'very clearly' they want to form a government with Labour. Their best-ever polls came in late September and early October 2026 (up to 16% in 1News–Verian); the 13 October Verian poll had them on 15%.

Key 2026 election policies

  • A tax package with a wealth tax, an inheritance tax and higher corporate tax, paired with income-tax cuts the party says would benefit 96% of people. [RNZ]
  • Free public transport for children and students and a $2 adult fare. A $4b plan to restore intercity passenger rail. [1News]
  • A National Electrification Plan announced during the 2026 fuel crisis. [RNZ]
  • Regulating artificial intelligence (12 October): no fully automated decisions in public services that make a substantive difference to people's lives, explicit consent standards for AI training data, and environmental impact assessments for hyperscale data centres. [RNZ]
  • Economic development plan (13 October): nearly $6b over four years; the party says it would create 284,000 jobs (64,000 above Treasury's forecast), including rescoping Investment Boost as 'Innovation Boost', Mission Fellowships, an Idea-to-Export pathway, restoring science funding toward 3% of GDP on R&D, and Kiwi-made public procurement. [RNZ]
  • Public service (15 October 2026): reverse National's $2.4b public service cuts at a cost of $3.17b over four years, by reversing the Budget 2026 2% baseline cuts and cancelling the further 5% cuts expected in each of the next two years. [RNZ]
  • A $30,000 cap on political donations, with donations from people only. [Pacific Media Network]
  • Bed levy (14 October 2026): give councils the power to set a levy on visitor accommodation to fund infrastructure, with a higher rate allowed on Airbnbs and other short-term rentals and a national register of short-term rentals. National has ruled out a bed levy. [RNZ]
  • Revoking mining consents approved under fast-track. A bill to protect conservation land from new mining. [Green Party]
  • Paid placements (16 October 2026): a universal $380 weekly payment for students on compulsory unpaid placements while training as nurses, teachers, social workers, midwives, vets, dentists and similar roles, plus a $1500 relocation payment. Costed at $311m over four years for about 10,000 students, funded from the proposed wealth tax. The Greens say their full fiscal plan comes out next week. [RNZ]
  • Apprentices (17 October 2026): abolish the training wage (now 80% of the adult minimum wage, $19.16 an hour) so apprentices and trainees get at least the minimum wage; a $2000 equipment grant for tools, uniforms and course materials; $30m for Māori and Pasifika-led training; and a $2000 completion bonus for employers. The Greens put the cost at $117.35m a year (costings verified by Infometrics). ACT would cut the training wage to 60%; Labour offers a $1000 tools grant and a two-year Apprenticeship Boost. Luxon, Peters and Seymour criticised the plan, and Hipkins said Labour would want to review the evidence first. [RNZ]

Notable 2025–26 events and controversies

  • Benjamin Doyle resigned from Parliament effective 3 October 2025, citing safety concerns. Mike Davidson replaced them (secondary source). [Wikipedia]
  • July 2026: Green MP Lawrence Xu-Nan was the target of Winston Peters' 'Go back to your own country' remark in Parliament. The Greens called it racist, and the Prime Minister criticised Peters. [RNZ]
  • Labour and the Greens held a joint press conference at Waitangi in February 2026 to signal they would work together. [ODT]
  • 13 October 2026: co-leader Chlöe Swarbrick took part in TVNZ's Multi-Party Debate with ACT, NZ First, Te Pāti Māori and Opportunity. She said the Greens want to change the government and are 'not interested in reinstalling Christopher Luxon as Prime Minister'. [1News]
  • 14 October 2026: a social media post by Mongrel Mob lifetime member Harry Tam showed Green candidates Hūhana Lyndon and Michel Mulipola (and two Te Pāti Māori MPs) at a whānau health and wellbeing election hui. National criticised it; Davidson said the Greens were 'unapologetic' about engaging with all communities. That night Hipkins ruled out law-and-order portfolios for Green or TPM MPs. [RNZ]
  • 15 October 2026: Davidson stood by the hui, saying the Greens do not endorse gang violence or crime and engage with community-led initiatives. She said the party would eventually repeal the gang patch ban but it was not a priority 'at all'. [RNZ]
  • 15 October 2026: Tamatha Paul, the Greens MP defending Wellington North, took part in the Aro Valley candidates' debate with Labour's Ayesha Verrall and candidates from ACT, NZ First, Opportunity and smaller parties. National's candidate pulled out at the last minute for health reasons. [RNZ]
