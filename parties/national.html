New Zealand National Party

Government (leads coalition)

In brief

History
Founded in 1936 from a merger of the United and Reform parties. It is New Zealand's main centre-right party and has led governments under Holland, Holyoake, Muldoon, Bolger/Shipley and Key/English. It won the most votes in 2023 and formed the current government with ACT and NZ First.
Ideology / positioning
Centre-right. Liberal-conservative, pro-market, and focused on fiscal restraint, law and order, and 'getting the basics right'.
Current role
Leads the Sixth National Government (since 27 November 2023) under separate coalition agreements with ACT and NZ First. Christopher Luxon is Prime Minister and Nicola Willis is Minister of Finance.

Key 2026 election policies

  • Plan for Kiwi Kids (21 October 2026, Luxon and Erica Stanford in Pongakawa): mostly existing policies (KiwiSaver BabyBoost, screens out of ECE, the school phone ban, replacing NCEA, keeping students at school to the end of Year 11). The one new policy expands the Maths Acceleration Programme so 40,000 more Year 7–10 students can take part over three years (75,900 are already funded for 2027–29); students must be at least a year behind the curriculum. [RNZ]
  • 'No new taxes': on 23 August 2026 Luxon widened the pledge from 'no new taxes on working people' and ruled out a bank tax and a bed (accommodation) tax. [RNZ]
  • Welfare: Jobseeker changes announced 8 October 2026, including random drug testing ('If you can work, you should'). [X]
  • Education: NCEA to be replaced by new qualifications (NZCE at Year 12, NZACE at Year 13). Details were confirmed in May 2026. [RNZ]
  • Planning: the Planning Act and Natural Environment Act replacing the RMA passed on 22 September 2026. The full system is intended to be running by 2030. [National Party]
  • Social media: a bill to ban under-16s from most social media platforms was introduced as a National Party bill in August 2026. ACT and NZ First oppose it (secondary source). [Wikipedia]
  • Fiscal plan (12 October 2026): tax relief only once the books are back in surplus (not forecast before 2029); a commitment to an independent election policy-costing unit by the 2029 election; $451.8m to extend the school lunch programme to the next election. [RNZ]
  • Superannuation: National confirmed its fiscal plan is its super policy. It still proposes raising the age gradually but sets no date, and commits to cross-party talks within 100 days of a new government. [RNZ]
  • Aid: a plan to cut aid outside the Pacific to fund health spending. Coalition partner NZ First publicly criticised it. [X]
  • Young New Zealanders (13 October 2026): five commitments to legislate or direct in the first 100 days: lower student loan repayment rates for borrowers living in New Zealand, wider access to 5% deposits for first-home buyers, paid parental leave extended from 26 to 30 weeks within three years, a $1500 KiwiSaver 'Baby Boost', and faster visas for partners of New Zealand citizens. National says each is funded to start by 1 July 2027. [National Party]
  • Plug-in solar (15 October 2026): shops able to sell plug-in solar panels within six months of a re-elected National government, once safety rules being developed with Australia are finished; a Home Energy Fund for rooftop solar, batteries, insulation and heat pumps, repaid through rates; and rooftop solar without a consent. [National Party]
  • Auckland plan (14 October 2026): remove the blanket ban on minimum car-parking requirements and let councils set minimums in suburban areas outside intensification zones, greenfield subdivisions and business parks. Most of the nine-page plan restates enacted or already announced policies. [RNZ]
  • Right to silence (18 October 2026, Paul Goldsmith): a new Crimes Act offence of not giving police information when a child under 12 is killed and it is clear one or more adults were present at or before the death, with a maximum of 10 years' prison. National says it would work with legal experts on a narrow exception to the right to silence. Child Matters said the idea needs careful scrutiny. [RNZ]
  • Waikato (19 October 2026, Chris Bishop): if re-elected, start construction of the SH1 Cambridge to Piarere extension of the Waikato Expressway early next year. Most of the release restates projects already under way or announced (Hamilton Southern Links, the Waikato medical school, fast-track approvals for Green Steel and Waihi North). [National Party]
  • Social media (19 October 2026): after Labour's announcement, Luxon matched its promise to progress an under-16 social media ban within a new government's first 100 days, saying it had always been his plan to prioritise it next term. [RNZ]
  • Agriculture manifesto (16 October 2026, AgFest in Greymouth): 19 sets of nationwide rules (national standards) so farmers can do work such as growing vegetables, storing water, managing animal waste, building farm tracks and housing seasonal workers without council consent if they meet the conditions. No timeline was given, and five of the 19 standards repeat earlier announcements. Also up to 70 graduate vets a year supported to work rurally from 2027 (up to $3.4m over four years) and $16m over four years for wild deer, goat and pest control from the international visitor levy. [RNZ]

Notable 2025–26 events and controversies

  • 21 October 2026, Bay of Plenty tour (Pongakawa, Edgecumbe, Whakatāne): Luxon was asked by his own supporters about Opportunity and again ruled it out ('I don't know how to be any clearer about it', to 1News). He told residents unhappy about Bay of Plenty toll roads that tolls would be part of 'our future going forward' and a toll strategy was coming next year, and told a resident who raised rising dairy-farm costs to 'be thankful' for record returns. [RNZ]
  • 21 October 2026: after Israel was announced as a Pacific Islands Forum partner, Luxon said it was a decision for the Forum and Israel would be a 'lower tier' partner. Labour's Vanushi Walters said Luxon had 'serious questions to answer'; Seymour welcomed the move; Swarbrick called it 'concerning'; Peters (22 October) said he respected the Pacific countries' decision. [RNZ]
  • 21 October 2026, Morning Report health debate: Simeon Brown said the health system was 'by no means' fixed but was 'heading in the right direction', citing a 41% fall in people waiting more than four months for elective surgery, and accused Labour of a 'fiscal cliff' in year four of its health funding, which Labour's Ayesha Verrall called 'a lie'. [RNZ]
  • 22 October 2026: in the NZ Herald's Mood of the Boardroom survey (about 150 chief executives and chairs, reported by RNZ), Erica Stanford was rated the top Cabinet minister (4.38 out of 5) and 74% rated Nicola Willis credible as finance minister. Luxon was again outside the top 10 (2.52 out of 5), but 65% picked him as the leader best able to drive growth, against 3% for Hipkins. [RNZ]
  • 21 October 2026: former National minister Jonathan Coleman told Midday Report that Luxon's 'premature' rule-out of Opportunity was a strategic error that had painted him into a corner. [RNZ]
  • 20 October 2026, final Stuff leaders' debate: Luxon first called the health system 'challenging', later accepting it was in crisis but saying he was focused on fixing it rather than debating terminology. He said he wants to raise the super age 'slowly, gradually' but through cross-party agreement, again ruled out Opportunity and called the question of whether he would resign rather than govern with it 'a ridiculous question'. On Hipkins' possible partners he said: 'You've got partners that want to be in coalition with the Mongrel Mob, my friend.' Afterwards he said 'I don't comment on polls'. [RNZ]
  • 20 October 2026: the New Zealand–India free trade agreement came into force, removing tariffs on more than half of New Zealand's exports to India. Luxon and Trade Minister Todd McClay marked it at the Ports of Auckland, and Luxon said he 'appreciated' Labour backing it. [RNZ]
  • 20 October 2026: Simeon Brown said Labour's emergency-department plan should be judged on its record, saying six-hour ED performance fell from about 89% (2017) to 68% (2023) and is now 76%, and challenged Hipkins to commit to keeping the health target. (On 21 October, in RNZ's Morning Report health debate, Labour said it would keep the health targets, according to RNZ's live blog.) [RNZ]
  • April 2026: after media reports of caucus discontent, Luxon called a confidence vote on his own leadership and won it (21 April). A reshuffle earlier that month moved the campaign-chair role from Chris Bishop to Simeon Brown. [RNZ]
  • August 2026: Chris Penk challenged Luxon. Luxon survived a second confidence vote on 12 August and stripped Penk of his portfolios. Penk later withdrew as a candidate. [1News]
  • September 2026: the Advertising Standards Authority partly upheld a complaint that a National attack ad on Labour's capital gains tax was misleading. [RNZ]
  • Budget 2026 (28 May) was billed as restrained. Willis said she had not convinced coalition partners to raise the NZ Super age. [RNZ]
  • Luxon has ruled out working with the Opportunity Party, and has called strategic vote-splitting 'insanely stupid'. [RNZ]
  • 13 October 2026: after a 1News–Verian poll tied National and Labour on 28% with Opportunity on 8%, Luxon again ruled out working with Opportunity and a grand coalition, and said it would be a 'very, very tight election'. [1News]
  • 14 October 2026, NZ Herald leaders' debate: Luxon again ruled out Opportunity. He said the minimum wage would rise with inflation ('that's what we've more or less been doing'), though RNZ notes it rose by less than inflation in each of the past three years, and said Foodstuffs would be split 'for sure', which the moderator noted depends on the Commerce Commission. [RNZ]
  • 14 October 2026: Winston Peters' office called National's claim that foreign aid had risen 26% since 2021 'incorrect', saying the inflation-adjusted rise was about 10%. National stands by its unadjusted figure. [RNZ]
  • 15 October 2026: Health Minister Simeon Brown rejected reports that cancer treatment at Starship children's hospital had been suspended because of nursing shortages, saying no child had missed out on care and that the eight vacant specialist nursing roles had been filled or were being recruited. A mother told RNZ her toddler's treatment had been delayed. [RNZ]
  • 14 October 2026: justice spokesperson Paul Goldsmith accused the Greens and Te Pāti Māori of 'prioritising' gangs over victims after their candidates attended a Mongrel Mob election hui. [RNZ]
  • 17 October 2026: RNZ checked Luxon's August claim that the government had 'certainly strengthened healthcare'. Nationally, 76.3% of emergency department patients were seen within six hours (March–June 2026), five points better than Labour's last figures in 2023, but about half of hospitals went backwards year on year and the figures predate this winter's flu outbreak. Simeon Brown said the results also showed where more work and investment were needed. [RNZ]
  • 18 October 2026: RNZ reported that Luxon chose 7 November although the Electoral Commission called it 'challenging, but manageable' (advance voting starts on Labour Day) and saw 17 October and 21 November as more feasible. Labour accused him of 'knowingly limiting turnout'. Luxon said 'We picked this date', and on 19 October laughed off claims of voter suppression, saying more people are enrolled now than at this stage of the last election. [RNZ]
  • 19 October 2026: lawyers criticised National's right-to-silence policy. Defence lawyer Marie Dyhrberg KC said it 'flies in the face of justice' and reverses the burden of proof; retired law professor Bill Hodge said it would change police interviews in a way he called 'dangerous', though he did not feel as strongly. [RNZ]
  • 19 October 2026: Luxon denied the health system was in crisis after Starship treatment delays and warnings from Wairau Hospital's emergency department, saying 'we are making progress' and pointing to emergency-department waiting times. Hipkins then challenged him to a debate on health; RNZ said it had approached National for comment. [RNZ]
  • 19 October 2026: at the Cambridge to Piarere Expressway site Luxon challenged NZTA chief executive Brett Gliddon to finish the $1.8b, five-year project in 18 months ('go 24/7'). Gliddon called that 'a bit ambitious'. Luxon later called it 'a coaching conversation', not criticism. [RNZ]
  • 19 October 2026: after Oriini Kaipara's comments about the Mongrel Mob, Luxon said gangs 'peddle misery and death and suffering', called the Greens and Te Pāti Māori MPs who visited the gang pad 'muppets', and urged centrist Labour voters to party-vote National. At a Hamilton rally he told supporters the Mongrel Mob would be part of a Labour-led coalition. [RNZ]
  • 17 October 2026: asked again about Opportunity, Luxon repeated that he had ruled it out and said he would not prefer opposition: 'we're going to win'. At Hobsonville's Catalina Bay market a breast cancer volunteer told him she disagreed with National's plan to fund more medicines by cutting foreign aid; Luxon said the plan takes 10% off the aid budget. [RNZ]
