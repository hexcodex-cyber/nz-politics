ACT New Zealand

Government (coalition partner)

In brief

History
Founded in 1993–94 by Roger Douglas and Derek Quigley, architects of 1980s economic reform. It has held Epsom since 2008. It grew from one MP to ten in 2020 and won 11 seats (8.64%) in 2023.
Ideology / positioning
Right. Classical liberal and libertarian-leaning: smaller government, lower taxes, deregulation, and 'equal rights' (opposing race-based policy).
Current role
Coalition partner in government. David Seymour has been Deputy Prime Minister since 31 May 2025 and is Minister for Regulation. The Regulatory Standards Act passed in November 2025.

Key 2026 election policies

  • Manifesto (26 October 2026, launched by Seymour in St Heliers, Auckland): brings together ACT's announced policies and promises $22b of cuts to 'wasteful' spending over four years, with government debt $12.4b lower by 2031 than forecast (health, education, defence, police and the Ministry for Children exempt from the efficiency drive, per an ACT press release). It drops ACT's September pledge to scrap the Permanent Resident Visa: existing PR holders are unaffected, but future holders would have to live in New Zealand at least two years in every five to keep it. It also includes the KiwiSaver earnings tax cut, raising the super age to 67 by 2035, electronic payment management for work-ready Jobseekers after four months, a three-year adult minimum wage freeze with a 60% training wage for under-20s, $400m more for GPs, a national cancer centre, and scrapping the Climate Change Commission and Zero Carbon Act. [RNZ]
  • Investing for school students (24 October 2026, Seymour): every Year 11 student gets $500 in a 'Sharesies-style' account, starting with a term deposit and moving to managed funds, NZ shares and international investments as they show they understand risk and reward. About $35m a year, which ACT says is costed in its fiscal plan; to be in ACT's manifesto, due on Monday 26 October. [ACT Party]
  • Charter schools (22 October 2026, Seymour in Henderson): a target of 10,000 charter school enrolments by 2029 and a charter school option in every major town, by converting more suitable state and state-integrated schools and encouraging new ones where there is demand and a sponsor. Labour has pledged to abolish the model. As Associate Education Minister, Seymour also announced that Cabinet had agreed to a legislative framework for nature-based early childhood education. [RNZ]
  • Trades (20 October 2026, Paul Henry and Laura McClure): an 'Industry Awards' qualification alongside University Entrance for students taking two industry-led subjects and a related academic subject; about $2000 per student for each approved industry-led subject (up to two a year); STAR, Gateway, Trades Academies and Vocational Pathways merged into one uncapped system; schools keep base funding when students learn off-site. ACT says it is funded within existing education baselines. [RNZ]
  • Fiscal plan (11 October 2026): return to 2017 per-capita spending; raise the NZ Super age by three months a year to 67 by 2035; cut disability benefits by $653m over four years via independent medical certification; abolish 'demographic' ministries; cut foreign aid (Pacific and Ukraine excluded). [RNZ]
  • Finish Treaty settlements and wind up the Waitangi Tribunal. [X]
  • Three Strikes for burglary, with a minimum three years' prison on a third strike. [RNZ]
  • Let teachers remove disruptive students and use 'reasonable force'. [RNZ]
  • A 'Working with Children Card' with continuous vetting. A Rural Workforce Visa. [RNZ]
  • Backs drug testing of beneficiaries, and says its energy policy overlaps with National's. [X]
  • Rental losses (14 October 2026): remove ring-fencing so landlords can offset rental losses against other taxable income. ACT puts the revenue cost at $264m in year one rising to $303m in year four, and says this is in its fiscal plan. [RNZ]
  • GPs (18 October 2026, announced by Tāmaki candidate James Christmas): an extra $100m a year for general practice over four years ($400m), on top of normal funding increases, to help practices keep and recruit doctors, plus streamlined registration for overseas-trained doctors. ACT says Labour's three free GP visits would make waits worse without more doctors. The General Practice Owners Association welcomed it. [RNZ]
  • Illegal dumping (16 October 2026, Andrew Hoggard): raise the infringement fee for illegal rubbish dumping from $400 to $1000, with $2000 fines for repeat offenders and higher court penalties for the most serious cases. [ACT Party]

Notable 2025–26 events and controversies

  • 28 October 2026: Seymour visited Santana Minerals' Bendigo-Ophir gold mine site near Cromwell and called the Greens' plan to reverse its fast-track consent 'economic terrorism' (RNZ live blog). The RNZ–Reid Research poll published the next morning had ACT on 10.1%, its best in that poll since 2023 and ahead of NZ First; Seymour called some of the poll's movements an 'outlier'. [RNZ]
  • 27 October 2026: after ACT reached a term-high 12% in the 1News–Verian poll, Seymour said 'we've laid out positive, practical solutions'. He toured Foodstuffs North Island's distribution centre, saying every other party wanted to 'beat up on business' over supermarkets; said Kerrin Leoni's independent bid had created a 'constitutional cluster'; defended private-sector health funding; and that evening told a Wānaka candidates' forum 'we should be open-minded' about private provision. [RNZ]
  • 26 October 2026: Seymour voted in Epsom after launching the manifesto. He dismissed Hipkins' promise of a government by Christmas, saying Hipkins would have to negotiate with Chlöe Swarbrick, whose party has 'never been in Cabinet'. On 27 October (RNZ First Up) he rejected links between abuse in state care and gang membership: 'Actually, people can choose.' [RNZ]
  • 25 October 2026, Auckland: Seymour said on gangs that 'we should help the people, not salute the institutions', and that people who did not enrol were showing 'dropkick behaviour'. On National's 100-day plan he would not pre-negotiate: 'There are some things that will go ahead, others that won't.' At a petrol station he argued against further fuel-price support beyond the Government's $50 for low-income families, saying it would mean more borrowing. [RNZ]
  • 23 October 2026: Seymour said Leoni's resignation showed that Labour 'doesn't really believe gangs are bad', asked whether she had raised money from gangs, and rejected claims of racism, pointing to the treatment of ACT's Karen Chhour and Nicole McKee. He called the Greens' fiscal plan 'a choice between financial security or financial ruin'. Speaking to health professionals in Auckland, he said ACT would expand the pharmacy minor-ailments scheme. [RNZ]
  • 22 October 2026: Seymour stood by his Powerbrokers' debate claim that some doctors sign people onto the sickness benefit when they do not need it, saying his evidence was people telling him it happened. Dr Luke Bradford, president of the Royal College of GPs, called the claim 'political rhetoric'. [RNZ]
  • 22 October 2026, Epsom candidates' debate: Seymour said Goldsmith was talking down National's chances and that Epsom 'belongs, and has always belonged, to the people of Epsom'. [RNZ]
  • 21 October 2026, Powerbrokers' debate: Seymour said he was no longer campaigning on the Treaty Principles Bill this election but expected the issue to return; said National's right-to-silence policy came from 'a very warm heart' but the details had not been thought through; defended the move-on orders law and clashed with Wong over Opportunity's land tax. [RNZ]
  • 21 October 2026: Seymour welcomed Israel's Pacific Islands Forum partnership ('a warm Pacific welcome') and said the 1News–Verian poll showed Labour voters 'abandoning the opposition in droves'. [RNZ]
  • 22 October 2026: an ACT spokesperson defended its KiwiSaver policy (scrap the government contribution and make investment earnings in super schemes tax-free) against Green-commissioned analysis that most of the benefit would go to the highest-income households. [RNZ]
  • 20 October 2026, Nelson public meeting: Seymour told voters to give their electorate vote to National's Blair Cameron rather than ACT's Chris Baillie, who was not seeking it (Labour's Rachel Boyack holds the seat by a handful of votes). He said emergency departments in the upper South Island 'resemble a war zone' and called Hipkins 'measurably the worst healthcare minister in New Zealand history'. [RNZ]
  • NZ First voted for ACT's Regulatory Standards Act at its third reading (13 November 2025), then vowed a week later to repeal it. Seymour accused Peters of bad faith. [RNZ]
  • ACT used 'agree to disagree' clauses on National's under-16 social media ban and opposed National–Labour modern slavery legislation (secondary source). [Wikipedia]
  • 12 October 2026: the Mental Health Foundation called ACT's proposed disability benefit cuts 'poorly researched' and 'based in bigotry'. Seymour says people who are genuinely disabled 'should be looked after well'. [RNZ]
  • Candidate Paul Henry's 'little shits in classrooms' remark when launching ACT's school-discipline policy drew criticism from Labour. [RNZ]
  • Nicole McKee became deputy leader at ACT's AGM in late June 2026 (secondary source). [Wikipedia]
  • 13 October 2026: David Seymour took part in TVNZ's Multi-Party Debate. He defended ACT's philanthropically backed cancer hospital plan and was the only leader on stage to back drug-testing beneficiaries. [1News]
  • 19 October 2026: deputy leader Nicole McKee responded to Oriini Kaipara's comments by saying the Mongrel Mob 'have no mana' and that victims of gang violence are the ones who deserve to have their mana restored. She restated ACT's plan to extend Three Strikes to burglary. [ACT Party]
  • 19 October 2026: Luxon said ACT was free to go into coalition with Opportunity, but that both would then be in opposition ('he can go with Opportunity, if he would wish, outside of government'). On 20 October Seymour told RNZ that ACT's job was to make sure the coalition is re-elected with 'more ACT in it', and that the real question about Opportunity was whether it would make it into Parliament. [RNZ]
  • 15 October 2026, Wānaka public meeting: Seymour said a 'four-headed beast' right-leaning bloc including Opportunity would be 'probably a good outcome' compared with a left-wing government, though he doubted Opportunity would reach Parliament. National has repeatedly ruled Opportunity out. He also called the India FTA a 'red herring' for immigration concerns. [NZ Herald]
