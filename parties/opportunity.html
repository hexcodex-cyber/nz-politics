The Opportunity Party (formerly TOP)

Not in Parliament. Polling around or above 5%

Outside Parliament: shorter profile

In brief

History
Founded in 2016 by economist Gareth Morgan as The Opportunities Party (TOP). It has never won a seat (2.22% in 2023). Qiulae Wong became leader in November 2025 and the party rebranded as The Opportunity Party.
Ideology / positioning
Self-described centrist, 'evidence-based' and radical centrist. Wong says supporters are 'politically homeless'. A Talbot Mills analysis found about a third of its supporters came from the left and a third from the right (secondary source).
Current role
Outside Parliament. The 13 October 1News–Verian poll put it on 8% (about 10 seats), the clearest kingmaker position in recent polling. Luxon has again ruled out working with it; ACT's David Seymour said on 15 October he would be open to a bloc including it. Wong says Opportunity will speak first to the largest party by seats on election night, and won't support a government with Winston Peters as foreign minister.

Key 2026 election policies

  • Five post-election bottom lines: a stronger Commerce Commission; lobbying regulation and donation caps; a 10-year health plan; phasing out bottom trawling; and recommitting to the Paris Agreement with an independent Climate Change Commission. [RNZ]
  • A land value tax (not one of the bottom lines), citizens' assemblies, and a methane ETS (secondary source). [Wikipedia]

Notable 2025–26 events and controversies

  • Luxon ruled out Opportunity in July 2026 and reaffirmed this at the 6 October debate. Wong says she does not believe him. [RNZ]
  • Peters called the party's candidates 'communists' and its bottom lines a 'wrinkly derrière'. Shane Jones called its policies 'not rational'. [Newsroom]
  • 13 October 2026: Qiulae Wong took part in TVNZ's Multi-Party Debate. She said Opportunity would negotiate first with the largest party and told the Greens they could have stopped 'egregious' coalition moves by working with National. [1News]
  • 15 October 2026: ACT's David Seymour said a coalition including Opportunity to keep a left-wing government out 'would be a good outcome', while doubting Opportunity would be the kingmaker. National has ruled Opportunity out on multiple occasions. [RNZ]
  • 14 October 2026, ASB finance debate: Wong joined Edmonds, Jones and Swarbrick in backing a bed tax for Queenstown, and backed a cross-party review of superannuation. [RNZ]
