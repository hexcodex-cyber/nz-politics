Te Pāti Māori

Opposition

In brief

History
Founded in 2004 (as the Māori Party) after the foreshore and seabed controversy. It supported National-led governments in 2008–17, was out of Parliament in 2017–20, and returned in 2020. In 2023 it won 6 of the 7 Māori electorates.
Ideology / positioning
Kaupapa Māori. Tino rangatiratanga, Te Tiriti o Waitangi, and generally left-leaning on economic policy.
Current role
Opposition. It is running a candidate-vote-only strategy: it asks Māori-roll voters for their electorate vote in all seven Māori seats, aims to create an overhang, and submitted a one-person party list.

Key 2026 election policies

  • Candidate-vote ('one tick') strategy across the seven Māori electorates, with a one-person list. [RNZ]
  • Tax: no tax on the first $30,000 of income. [1News]
  • Housing: 50% of new public housing builds ring-fenced for Māori. [1News]
  • A permanent ban on seabed mining (Ngarewa-Packer's member's bill). [Scoop]
  • Toi Māori (12 October 2026, Ōtautahi): an independent Toi Māori Authority, separate from Creative New Zealand and funded at parity with it, and a rotating two-year income programme paying 1000 Māori artists at a time for 30 hours' work a week. [Scoop]

Notable 2025–26 events and controversies

  • 23 October 2026 (reported 24 October): Waititi sought to distance Te Pāti Māori from its role in Kerrin Leoni's downfall, saying 'the right benefits every time we turn on each other' and that the controversy would not distract from the 'collective responsibility to dismantle this Government'. [newstalkzb.co.nz]
  • 23 October 2026: after Labour's Kerrin Leoni resigned as a candidate in Tāmaki Makaurau (her name stays on the ballot), Waititi called it 'a sad day for Māori', accused the right of exploiting the issue, and urged voters to give their electorate vote to Te Pāti Māori and party vote to Labour or the Greens. Former co-leader Te Ururoa Flavell told Checkpoint her resignation was inevitable because she had not been upfront with Labour, but that meeting gang members was part of an MP's job. [RNZ]
  • A Whakaata Māori–Curia poll of Tāmaki Makaurau (5–15 Oct, published 20 Oct; 500 Māori-roll voters, ±4.5%) had Oriini Kaipara on 33%, Labour's Kerrin Leoni 31% and the Greens' Florence Te Ohaere 21% (9% unsure), a gap within the margin of error. Party vote: Labour 28%, Greens 17%, TPM 10%, National 6%. 35% rated Kaipara's performance as good and 13% as poor. The earlier Mata–Horizon poll had Leoni ahead. [Te Ao Māori News]
  • 21 October 2026: co-leader Debbie Ngarewa-Packer's mother, Colleen Ngarewa, died at the whānau home in Pātea; Ngarewa-Packer had stepped back from the campaign last month while she was unwell. Te Pāti Māori had pulled out of that night's NZ Herald 'Powerbrokers' debate, first citing 'significant whānau and prior electorate commitments'. [RNZ]
  • 2025: the Privileges Committee punished the co-leaders (21 days' suspension) and Maipi-Clarke (7 days) over the November 2024 haka in the Treaty Principles Bill vote. Parliament confirmed this on 5 June 2025 (secondary source). [Wikipedia]
  • November 2025: MPs Mariameno Kapa-Kingi and Tākuta Ferris were expelled. The High Court reinstated Kapa-Kingi in December 2025. In May 2026 she left to found the Te Tai Tokerau Party. Court evidence about the party's handling was heavily reported. [The Spinoff]
  • Waitangi 2026: Waititi publicly apologised to the Kapa-Kingi whānau and Ngāpuhi ('If I have done you wrong, I offer you my head'). [RNZ]
  • Oriini Kaipara won the Tāmaki Makaurau by-election (September 2025). In May 2026 she denied a report that she was considering leaving. [RNZ]
  • A Whakaata Māori–Curia poll (21 Sep–1 Oct) had Waititi on 42% in Waiariki (52% excluding undecided). In the same poll the party vote was Labour 24%, TPM 23% and Greens 22%. [RNZ]
  • 13 October 2026: Rawiri Waititi took part in TVNZ's Multi-Party Debate (without co-leader Debbie Ngarewa-Packer). He defended compulsory te reo Māori in schools and quipped 'You poor thing' after Peters complained about Māori City Rail Link station names. [1News]
  • 14 October 2026: Oriini Kaipara and Hana-Rāwhiti Maipi-Clarke appeared in photos from a Mongrel Mob election hui. A party spokesperson said: 'We do not judge, they have whakapapa… We are unapologetic about that.' [RNZ]
  • A Whakaata Māori–Curia poll of Ikaroa-Rāwhiti (28 Sep–8 Oct, published 13 Oct; 500 Māori-roll voters) had Labour MP Cushla Tangaere-Manuel on 40%, TPM's Haley Maxwell on 19% and the Greens' Heather Te Au-Skipworth on 15% (20% unsure). Party vote: Labour 41%, Greens 25%, TPM 10%. [Te Ao Māori News]
  • 18 October 2026, TVNZ Q+A: independent Te Tai Tonga MP Tākuta Ferris (expelled from Te Pāti Māori in 2025) said he would not support a Luxon-led government and wants a left-led one, but did not say what he would do if the left falls short. He confirmed he and Mariameno Kapa-Kingi would keep caucusing together if both are re-elected. [1News]
  • A Mata–Horizon poll of Māori voters (electorate questions 25 Sep–9 Oct, 711 respondents, published 17 Oct) had Labour candidates ahead in four Māori seats, including Te Tai Tokerau (Willow-Jean Prime 27%, the Greens' Hūhana Lyndon 23%, TPM's Aperahama Edwards 19%, Mariameno Kapa-Kingi 11%) and Tāmaki Makaurau, where Labour's Kerrin Leoni led Oriini Kaipara 38% to 30% (margin of error ±8.1). Maipi-Clarke and Ngarewa-Packer led in their seats and independent Tākuta Ferris in Te Tai Tonga. RNZ says the electorate samples are small and the results are indications only; the earlier Whakaata Māori–Curia poll had Waititi well ahead in Waiariki, where this poll put Labour's Toni Boynton in front. [RNZ]
  • 19 October 2026: Oriini Kaipara doubled down on her promise to 'restore mana' to Mongrel Mob members, telling RNZ's Midday Report 'you see Mongrel Mob, I see whānau' and that gangs exist because of 'systemic failures', including abuse in state care. Hipkins, Luxon, ACT's Nicole McKee and National's Paul Goldsmith rejected her comments, and Marama Davidson said they were for Te Pāti Māori to answer to. [RNZ]
  • A Whakaata Māori–Curia poll of Te Tai Tokerau (5–15 Oct, published 19 Oct; 500 Māori-roll voters, ±4.5%) had the Greens' Hūhana Lyndon on 24%, Labour's Willow-Jean Prime 18%, TPM's Aperahama Edwards 17% and incumbent Mariameno Kapa-Kingi 12% (22% unsure). Party vote: Labour 27%, Greens 25%, TPM 11%, NZ First 6%, National 4%, Te Tai Tokerau Party and Opportunity 3% each. The earlier Mata–Horizon poll had Prime ahead in this seat. No Green candidate has ever won a Māori electorate. [Te Ao Māori News]
