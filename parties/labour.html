New Zealand Labour Party

Opposition (Official Opposition)

In brief

History
Founded in 1916 and New Zealand's oldest current party. It built the welfare state under Savage (1935) and has led governments under Fraser, Nash, Kirk, Lange, Clark and Ardern/Hipkins. It lost power in 2023 after two terms.
Ideology / positioning
Centre-left. Social democratic.
Current role
Official Opposition. Chris Hipkins (Prime Minister January to November 2023) is leader and is seeking to form a government with the Greens and, potentially, Te Pāti Māori and/or Opportunity.

Key 2026 election policies

  • A capital gains tax on property only (28%, excluding the family home and farms, from July 2027) to fund three free GP visits a year for everyone through a 'Medicard'. [RNZ]
  • An 'NZ Future Fund' modelled on Singapore's Temasek, announced October 2025 (secondary source). [Wikipedia]
  • A 10% write-off of eligible student loan balances, announced at the 27 September campaign launch. [RNZ]
  • A $160m 'SolarSaver' package with subsidies and loans for rooftop and plug-in solar. [RNZ]
  • Visa-waiver travel for Pacific Islands Forum citizens. [Asia Pacific Report]

Notable 2025–26 events and controversies

  • Reversal on capital gains tax: in July 2023 Hipkins ruled out a CGT 'End of story'. In October 2025 Labour adopted a property CGT. [RNZ]
  • National and Labour co-sponsored modern slavery legislation (January 2026), over ACT's opposition. Labour also backed the NZ–India FTA, which NZ First opposed (secondary source). [Wikipedia]
  • Labour's costings were attacked by National ('the least credible fiscal plan… in living memory'). Labour says its plan is fully costed. [National Party]
  • 22 September 2026: list-only candidate Warrick Cleine withdrew after controversy over the fact that he does not live in New Zealand (secondary source). [Wikipedia]
  • Labour is contesting the Māori seats hard against Te Pāti Māori, and has criticised TPM's candidate-vote-only strategy. [RNZ]
