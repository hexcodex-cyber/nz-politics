New Zealand Labour Party

Opposition (Official Opposition)

In brief

History
Founded in 1916 and New Zealand's oldest current party. It built the welfare state under Savage (1935) and has led governments under Fraser, Nash, Kirk, Lange, Clark and Ardern/Hipkins. It lost power in 2023 after two terms.
Ideology / positioning
Centre-left. Social democratic.
Current role
Official Opposition. Chris Hipkins (Prime Minister January to November 2023) is leader and is seeking to form a government with the Greens and, potentially, Te Pāti Māori and/or Opportunity.

Key 2026 election policies

  • A capital gains tax on property only (28%, excluding the family home and farms, from July 2027) to fund three free GP visits a year for everyone through a 'Medicard'. [RNZ]
  • An 'NZ Future Fund' modelled on Singapore's Temasek, announced October 2025 (secondary source). [Wikipedia]
  • A 10% write-off of eligible student loan balances, announced at the 27 September campaign launch. [RNZ]
  • A $160m 'SolarSaver' package with subsidies and loans for rooftop and plug-in solar. [RNZ]
  • Cheaper school uniforms (limits on compulsory branded items and generic alternatives allowed), announced 12 October, alongside locally made school lunches from Term 1 2028 and restoring 43 cancelled rural school bus routes 'where possible'. [RNZ]
  • Local government (15 October 2026, LGNZ conference): Hipkins said Labour would expand its proposed ratepayer assistance scheme to cover rates and development contributions, and that 'everything should be on the table' for council funding, including visitor levies. [1News]
  • Visa-waiver travel for Pacific Islands Forum citizens. [Asia Pacific Report]
  • Economic plan (14 October 2026): redirect up to $50m a year of the government's NZ Super Fund contribution into the Elevate venture capital fund; make big businesses pay small suppliers within 15 days; immediate write-off of new tools and equipment up to $10,000; raise the GST threshold to $80,000; back 30,000 apprentices with a $1000 tools grant; surplus in 2028/29, restore the Reserve Bank's dual mandate and set up an independent Parliamentary Budget Office. [RNZ]

Notable 2025–26 events and controversies

  • Reversal on capital gains tax: in July 2023 Hipkins ruled out a CGT 'End of story'. In October 2025 Labour adopted a property CGT. [RNZ]
  • National and Labour co-sponsored modern slavery legislation (January 2026), over ACT's opposition. Labour also backed the NZ–India FTA, which NZ First opposed (secondary source). [Wikipedia]
  • Labour's costings were attacked by National ('the least credible fiscal plan… in living memory'). Labour says its plan is fully costed. [National Party]
  • 22 September 2026: list-only candidate Warrick Cleine withdrew after controversy over the fact that he does not live in New Zealand (secondary source). [Wikipedia]
  • Labour is contesting the Māori seats hard against Te Pāti Māori, and has criticised TPM's candidate-vote-only strategy. [RNZ]
  • 14 October 2026, NZ Herald leaders' debate: Hipkins ruled out giving law-and-order portfolios to any Green or Te Pāti Māori MP after their candidates were photographed with Mongrel Mob members, and said Chlöe Swarbrick could never be Treasurer (RNZ's wording). In a quick-fire round he put a lower voting age, a rainbow affairs ministry and compulsory te reo 'on the table', and ruled out binding Waitangi Tribunal recommendations and a higher corporate tax. [RNZ]
  • 15 October 2026: Hipkins called the Green and Te Pāti Māori candidates' Mongrel Mob photos 'really, really ill considered' and said any potential minister of his who posed with gang members 'wouldn't be a member of the cabinet'. Willie Jackson said the meeting was not a good strategic move. Davidson stood by it. [RNZ]
  • 15 October 2026: at a Kelston rally Hipkins used Labour's new attack line that Luxon has left people $6000 worse off (Labour's own calculation, which it has also published as an online calculator). [RNZ]
  • 14 October 2026, ASB finance debate: Edmonds and Willis clashed over Investment Boost, which Labour would replace with a policy targeting small businesses. Both back cross-party talks on superannuation. [RNZ]
  • 15 October 2026, LGNZ conference in Rotorua: Hipkins said councils had been left 'in limbo' by the government's paused Head Start amalgamation programme. He said Labour would keep pursuing local government reform but with more community input and without 'arbitrary deadlines', and voiced reservations about abolishing regional councils. Local Government Minister Simon Watts ruled out reimbursing councils for their Head Start costs. [RNZ]
  • 17 October 2026: asked about campaign chair Kieran McAnulty saying he would rather work with Opportunity than Te Pāti Māori, Hipkins said those were 'conversations for after the election' and that, for now, a vote for Opportunity risked re-electing the current government. At a Papatoetoe meeting of ethnic community leaders he said a minister of his making comments like those of Winston Peters and Shane Jones would be sacked, and that Labour would not fund any gang programmes in government. [RNZ]
