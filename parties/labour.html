New Zealand Labour Party

Opposition (Official Opposition)

In brief

History
Founded in 1916 and New Zealand's oldest current party. It built the welfare state under Savage (1935) and has led governments under Fraser, Nash, Kirk, Lange, Clark and Ardern/Hipkins. It lost power in 2023 after two terms.
Ideology / positioning
Centre-left. Social democratic.
Current role
Official Opposition. Chris Hipkins (Prime Minister January to November 2023) is leader and is seeking to form a government with the Greens and, potentially, Te Pāti Māori and/or Opportunity.

Key 2026 election policies

  • First-week plan (26 October 2026, Hipkins after voting in Upper Hutt): a commitment to form a Labour-led government before Christmas, with five immediate Cabinet actions: end the 'Health New Zealand hiring freeze' and start the job guarantee for NZ-trained graduate nurses; launch an Emergency Department Recovery Plan; start work on a Medicard (three free GP visits and free prescriptions a year); approve a three-year fuel tax freeze, progress public transport fare caps ($20 a week in Auckland, Wellington and Christchurch, $10 elsewhere) and prepare a 10% student loan balance cut from 1 July 2027; and agree legislation against price gouging on groceries, power and petrol and hidden fees, plus meet supermarket leaders on separating wholesale and retail. [RNZ]
  • Workers' plan (26 October 2026, Labour Day; Jan Tinetti press release): lift the minimum wage in line with inflation; work towards the Living Wage for public sector workers; restore pay equity and an immediate $4 an hour rise for care and support workers; bring back Fair Pay Agreements; reverse National's annual leave reductions; a new crime of corporate homicide; and protect collective bargaining. [Scoop]
  • Teacher aides (22 October 2026, Ginny Andersen): fund 1000 more teacher aides at a cost of $80m over four years and build on a 2022 review to close gaps in learning support. National's Erica Stanford said the government's $750m learning-support investment was already delivering more teacher-aide hours. [RNZ]
  • Emergency departments (20 October 2026, Hipkins and Ayesha Verrall): on day one, order an immediate report on hospital beds that could be opened with more staff and on deaths and safety linked to long ED waits, and restart winter planning; reporting against the emergency medicine college's bed-access targets; move vaccine-schedule decisions from Pharmac to the Public Health Agency; more extended-care paramedics; supported early discharge for medically stable older people; and a guaranteed job for every eligible New Zealand-trained graduate nurse (about 2400 a year, from 2027). Labour says capital gains tax revenue is ringfenced for health. [RNZ]
  • A capital gains tax on property only (28%, excluding the family home and farms, from July 2027) to fund three free GP visits a year for everyone through a 'Medicard'. [RNZ]
  • An 'NZ Future Fund' modelled on Singapore's Temasek, announced October 2025 (secondary source). [Wikipedia]
  • A 10% write-off of eligible student loan balances, announced at the 27 September campaign launch. [RNZ]
  • A $160m 'SolarSaver' package with subsidies and loans for rooftop and plug-in solar. [RNZ]
  • Cheaper school uniforms (limits on compulsory branded items and generic alternatives allowed), announced 12 October, alongside locally made school lunches from Term 1 2028 and restoring 43 cancelled rural school bus routes 'where possible'. [RNZ]
  • Local government (15 October 2026, LGNZ conference): Hipkins said Labour would expand its proposed ratepayer assistance scheme to cover rates and development contributions, and that 'everything should be on the table' for council funding, including visitor levies. [1News]
  • Kids Off Screens (18 October 2026): $7m a year for children's sport and after-school activities ($3m to keep the Active Me – Kia Tū grants of up to $300 a year, and $4m through Tū Manawa Active Aotearoa for community groups); updated screen-use guidance for early childhood and schools, with school boards required to set screen-use policies; keep the school phone ban. [RNZ]
  • Online safety (19 October 2026, Hipkins and Reuben Davidson): get the under-16 social media ban bill to select committee within the first 100 days; a 'Digital Duty of Care' law within the first year making platforms identify and fix risks (bullying, self-harm content, deepfakes, 'nudify' apps); an independent online regulator able to fine companies; and a choice between algorithm-driven and following-only feeds. (RNZ corrected an earlier report that Labour would implement the ban within 100 days.) Peters called it 'a naive solution to a genuine issue'. [RNZ]
  • Visa-waiver travel for Pacific Islands Forum citizens. [Asia Pacific Report]
  • Economic plan (14 October 2026): redirect up to $50m a year of the government's NZ Super Fund contribution into the Elevate venture capital fund; make big businesses pay small suppliers within 15 days; immediate write-off of new tools and equipment up to $10,000; raise the GST threshold to $80,000; back 30,000 apprentices with a $1000 tools grant; surplus in 2028/29, restore the Reserve Bank's dual mandate and set up an independent Parliamentary Budget Office. [RNZ]

Notable 2025–26 events and controversies

  • 26 October 2026: Hipkins voted at Silverstream School, Upper Hutt, then rallied about 100 supporters at Mt Cook School, Wellington, telling them National wanted people 'to vote based on their fears' while he wanted them to vote 'based on hope'. He said Kerrin Leoni, now running as an independent in Tāmaki Makaurau, 'will not be a Labour MP'. Campaign chair Kieran McAnulty challenged Luxon to say what National would cut to meet ACT's $22b savings plan; National campaign manager Simeon Brown replied that Hipkins had yet to explain how he would pay for $18b of new spending. [RNZ]
  • 26 October 2026: Kerrin Leoni, who resigned as Labour's Tāmaki Makaurau candidate on 23 October, said she will stay in the race and sit as an independent MP if elected. Her name stays on the ballot beside Labour's logo and she stays on Labour's list; the Electoral Commission said a vote for her is valid. Whether Labour could use the party-hopping law against her has not been tested (Te Ao Māori News via RNZ). [RNZ]
  • 25 October 2026, Wellington: Hipkins dismissed National's 100-day plan as 'rehashing' and said half of it was unfulfilled 2023 promises; he said National was 'desperate' to talk about gangs and law and order rather than the cost of living, health and jobs. He urged the more than 300,000 unenrolled people to enrol and said that falling short of 2023's enrolment rate would be 'an indictment on this Government'. Deputy leader Carmel Sepuloni reported people struggling with the online enrolment form. [RNZ]
  • 24 October 2026, Queensgate Mall, Lower Hutt: Hipkins said Luxon had made gangs 'a huge issue' and accused him of a 'massive double standard' over the Tama Potaka photo. Asked whether he had been photographed with gang members he said 'quite possibly' and that he could not recall a photo posted by Mongrel Mob life member Harry Tam of him with a man Tam says is a gang member (not wearing a patch). He said the Leoni affair had 'absolutely not' tanked Labour's campaign, and restated Labour's cost of living action plan (first released 11 October). [RNZ]
  • 25 October 2026 (NZ Herald via Newstalk ZB): the Herald reported that Hipkins' office had asked Health NZ for a Starship hospital visit before he said on 22 October that meeting hospital leaders during the campaign would be 'inappropriate'. Labour said his remarks 'could have been clearer' and meant Labour would not offer fixes from Opposition. Health NZ said no children are being treated in corridors at Starship; Hipkins had spoken of children treated 'in hallways, in waiting rooms'. [newstalkzb.co.nz]
  • 23 October 2026: Kerrin Leoni resigned as Labour's Tāmaki Makaurau candidate after further footage and a photo emerged of her with Mongrel Mob members at an early-October event. Hipkins said she had shown 'very very bad judgement', had not given the party full information, and that her position was 'untenable'; he also said there was 'an element of racism' in the reaction. Because nominations have closed, her name stays on the ballot as Labour's candidate. Leoni said a whānau function had been 'turned into a political weapon' and 'I will be back'. Willie Jackson called it a 'sad day' for Labour's Māori campaign. [RNZ]
  • 23 October 2026: Labour said police had confirmed that its candidate Rakesh Naidoo, a police superintendent, is not under investigation. Police Minister Mark Mitchell said it remained 'an ongoing matter for police'. [RNZ]
  • 22–23 October 2026: a video seen by RNZ showed Labour's Tāmaki Makaurau candidate Kerrin Leoni, in a Labour jacket, handing out flyers and telling Mongrel Mob members they 'have the power to change this government'. Leoni said it was a whānau function in early October, not a gang pad, and that she had not visited a pad during the campaign. Hipkins, who had called the Green and Te Pāti Māori gang-pad visit a 'real error of judgement', said he accepted her assurance but did not expect candidates to campaign in gang pads. Te Pāti Māori's Oriini Kaipara had raised the claim in a Whakaata Māori debate. [RNZ]
  • 22 October 2026, Hawke's Bay Chamber of Commerce: Hipkins said a Labour government would not stop projects already underway and funded, with a few exceptions such as the LNG import facility, criticised the cancellation of the new Interislander ferries, promised an independent curriculum centre, and said tackling drug addiction was the way to be tough on gangs. [RNZ]
  • 22 October 2026: after the CPI release, Willow-Jean Prime said 30,000 more people were on Jobseeker despite Luxon's promise to cut numbers by 50,000. Defence spokesperson Damien O'Connor told a Fabian Society meeting (21 October) that Labour would not engage on Ukraine's offer to co-develop military drones and questioned the funding for the $12b defence capability plan; National's Paul Goldsmith said officials were still advising on the drone offer. [RNZ]
  • 21 October 2026: Hipkins said Labour's fall in the 1News–Verian poll was 'only on the TV1 poll', pointing to The Post/Freshwater poll. Campaign chair Kieran McAnulty predicted 'a correction' in 1News' next poll, said neither bloc could govern without Opportunity and that Labour would not nudge its supporters towards it ('We don't do deals'). [1News]
  • 21 October 2026, Morning Report health debate: Ayesha Verrall confirmed Labour would keep the coalition's health targets and said some of its three free GP visits could be with a nurse practitioner. [RNZ]
  • 21 October 2026: visiting an aluminium plant in Hamilton, Hipkins said the government was wrong to reject interim import safeguards for the aluminium industry, but would not commit to reversing the decision, saying Labour would 'figure out a way forward' urgently. McAnulty also said the Electoral Commission should record how many people are turned away for not being enrolled. [RNZ]
  • 20 October 2026: Hipkins told Morning Report the former Labour government's $2.75m for the Mongrel Mob-run Kahukura meth-rehabilitation programme 'was a mistake', saying addiction treatment should be funded but not through gangs. Jacinda Ardern had defended the funding in 2021. [RNZ]
  • 20 October 2026: Hipkins said 'Winston Peters has had his time' after Peters said Hipkins should be jailed over the Covid-19 vaccine rollout. Peters then claimed Labour had approached NZ First staff 'months and months ago' about coalition options; Hipkins said 'Absolutely not'. [RNZ]
  • 20 October 2026, final Stuff leaders' debate: Hipkins said the health system is in crisis and Luxon was 'in denial', promised to end corridor care, said Labour would keep the super age at 65, said he was open to talking to Opportunity although 'it's unclear yet exactly what it is that they stand for', and repeated that Green and TPM MPs would not hold law-and-order portfolios. Afterwards he called the 1News–Verian poll an outlier: 'The mood on the ground that we're getting is very, very different to that.' [RNZ]
  • Reversal on capital gains tax: in July 2023 Hipkins ruled out a CGT 'End of story'. In October 2025 Labour adopted a property CGT. [RNZ]
  • National and Labour co-sponsored modern slavery legislation (January 2026), over ACT's opposition. Labour also backed the NZ–India FTA, which NZ First opposed (secondary source). [Wikipedia]
  • Labour's costings were attacked by National ('the least credible fiscal plan… in living memory'). Labour says its plan is fully costed. [National Party]
  • 22 September 2026: list-only candidate Warrick Cleine withdrew after controversy over the fact that he does not live in New Zealand (secondary source). [Wikipedia]
  • Labour is contesting the Māori seats hard against Te Pāti Māori, and has criticised TPM's candidate-vote-only strategy. [RNZ]
  • 14 October 2026, NZ Herald leaders' debate: Hipkins ruled out giving law-and-order portfolios to any Green or Te Pāti Māori MP after their candidates were photographed with Mongrel Mob members, and said Chlöe Swarbrick could never be Treasurer (RNZ's wording). In a quick-fire round he put a lower voting age, a rainbow affairs ministry and compulsory te reo 'on the table', and ruled out binding Waitangi Tribunal recommendations and a higher corporate tax. [RNZ]
  • 15 October 2026: Hipkins called the Green and Te Pāti Māori candidates' Mongrel Mob photos 'really, really ill considered' and said any potential minister of his who posed with gang members 'wouldn't be a member of the cabinet'. Willie Jackson said the meeting was not a good strategic move. Davidson stood by it. [RNZ]
  • 15 October 2026: at a Kelston rally Hipkins used Labour's new attack line that Luxon has left people $6000 worse off (Labour's own calculation, which it has also published as an online calculator). [RNZ]
  • 14 October 2026, ASB finance debate: Edmonds and Willis clashed over Investment Boost, which Labour would replace with a policy targeting small businesses. Both back cross-party talks on superannuation. [RNZ]
  • 15 October 2026, LGNZ conference in Rotorua: Hipkins said councils had been left 'in limbo' by the government's paused Head Start amalgamation programme. He said Labour would keep pursuing local government reform but with more community input and without 'arbitrary deadlines', and voiced reservations about abolishing regional councils. Local Government Minister Simon Watts ruled out reimbursing councils for their Head Start costs. [RNZ]
  • 18 October 2026, TVNZ Q+A: Hipkins called the end of same-day enrolment 'reckless' and said the Electoral Commission had 'absolutely not' done enough to warn voters, but said Labour would accept the election result. He said he wanted to look at moving the Social Investment Agency into the Prime Minister's department, and was 'very open' to discussing legislated minimum safe staffing levels in hospitals. [1News]
  • 19 October 2026: Hipkins rejected Oriini Kaipara's promise to 'restore mana' to Mongrel Mob members, calling gangs 'a scourge on society', and said Labour would not support changes to the gang patch ban. Health spokesperson Ayesha Verrall said Labour would end any hiring freeze and prioritise staffing oncology at Starship. [RNZ]
  • 19 October 2026: Hipkins challenged Luxon to a debate on health in a social media video, saying the health system was 'in crisis'. On coalition timing he would not promise a deal before Christmas: 'If you want a good, decisive outcome, vote for one. Vote Labour.' [RNZ]
  • 17 October 2026: asked about campaign chair Kieran McAnulty saying he would rather work with Opportunity than Te Pāti Māori, Hipkins said those were 'conversations for after the election' and that, for now, a vote for Opportunity risked re-electing the current government. At a Papatoetoe meeting of ethnic community leaders he said a minister of his making comments like those of Winston Peters and Shane Jones would be sacked, and that Labour would not fund any gang programmes in government. [RNZ]
