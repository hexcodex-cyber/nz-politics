Sources & methodology

Scope

Covered: the six parties with MPs when the 54th Parliament dissolved on 1 October 2026 (National, Labour, Greens, ACT, NZ First, Te Pāti Māori), plus the Opportunity Party. Opportunity is outside Parliament but polling around or above the 5% threshold, so it gets a shorter profile that is clearly marked. Te Tai Tokerau Party is noted but not profiled.

How the 'top 4' were chosen

Leader(s) plus the next four people, in this order of preference:

  1. The next places on the party's published 2026 list (National, Labour, ACT, NZ First, Greens' final list).
  2. Where a list doesn't tell you who matters (Te Pāti Māori's list has only one name), we used the other sitting MPs plus key party officers.

This means some people profiled are not current MPs: Paul Henry (ACT, rank 4), Alfred Ngaro (NZ First, rank 5), John Tamihere and Lance Norman (Te Pāti Māori officers), and Opportunity's leaders. The reasoning for each party is on its page.

Quote rules

  • Every quote comes from a page we actually retrieved during research (8–20 October 2026). Wording is as published by that source, including the source's own transcription.
  • Where a quote is relayed by a news outlet, we link that outlet. Where a release was republished (for example via Scoop), we say so.
  • X posts link to the post itself. Dates are converted to NZ time. Some long posts were only partly returned by the X API, so we quote the opening text.
  • Up to 8 quotes per person, newest first, from 2025–26. If fewer than 4 could be verified, the page says so. We did not pad with weak or uncertain material.
  • Context lines are our neutral description. We give no commentary on whether claims are true. 'Reversals' lists only cases where a source documents a statement and a later, different statement or action.
  • Dates are publication or post dates (NZ time). Where an event date was approximate, the context says so.

Polling

The Post/Freshwater Strategy poll (published 20 October) was checked against RNZ's live report and the Democracy Project's summary because The Post's article is paywalled. The two differ on Labour (29.7% v 28.7%); we show 28.7%, which matches RNZ's own description of a gap of just over one point, and mark it ‡. Other polls in the table were checked against the publishers: 1News–Verian (8–12 Oct, published 13 Oct; and 1–5 Oct) and RNZ–Reid Research (24 Sep–1 Oct) against their own articles, Taxpayers' Union–Curia (1–5 Oct) against RNZ's 8 October report, and Roy Morgan (31 Aug–27 Sep) against Roy Morgan's 6 October release (Curia and Roy Morgan checked 13 Oct 2026; latest Verian checked 14 Oct 2026).

Known gaps and uncertainties

  • People with fewer than 4 verified quotes: Lance Norman (2).
  • Background facts (careers, years elected) are summarised from Wikipedia and general knowledge of the public record, and each links to its Wikipedia page. Some party-level facts are marked 'secondary source' where only Wikipedia was used.
  • Portfolios are as listed for the caretaker ministry after Parliament's dissolution (main portfolios only, not every associate role).
  • Enrolment closing date (midnight Sunday 25 October 2026) was confirmed on 13 Oct 2026 against govt.nz (Electoral Commission key dates). Vote.nz key-dates still blocks automated fetches.
  • Paywalled NZ Herald and The Post articles were generally not used for quotes because we could not read them in full.
