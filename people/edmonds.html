Barbara Edmonds

Labour: Finance spokesperson

What they've actually said

Direct quotes from 2025–26, newest first. Context lines are ours. Wording is as published in the linked source.

  1. “It's around supporting them to make things easier for them to run their business, so they get paid on time and to make sure that they get a bigger cut of government business, because the government procures about $50 billion worth of goods and services.”

    14 October 2026 · On Labour's small-business focus, ahead of the ASB finance spokesperson debate. · RNZ

  2. “Not Chlöe Swarbrick and not Nicola Willis - Barbara Edmonds the MP for Kenepuru will be.”

    14 October 2026 · ASB finance debate in Queenstown, 14 Oct evening, as reported by RNZ. Opening remarks, on who would be the next finance minister. · RNZ

  3. “I don't have to copy Nicola, we are two very different people.”

    14 October 2026 · ASB finance debate in Queenstown, 14 Oct evening, as reported by RNZ. Asked what businesses that use Investment Boost would do if Labour scrapped it. · RNZ

  4. “Three years on from National's promise to get things moving, 171,000 Kiwis are out of work, business liquidations are at a 15-year high, and 2000 people are leaving the country every week. National had its chance, and families are worse off for it.”

    14 October 2026 · Launching Labour's economic plan (venture capital, small-business and apprenticeship measures). · RNZ

  5. “You need to have the strong mandate to do it. They wanted the mandate to do it, they could have put it in their policy ... that conversation still continues, even if they're in government, [or] we're in government.”

    13 October 2026 · Responding to National's superannuation stance; Labour would keep the age at 65 (reported by RNZ) · RNZ

  6. “We get about 3.1% on average over the forecast period. So we believe the 3% is conservative, and we think is prudent given that the forecasts do change...”

    7 October 2026 · interest.co.nz interview defending the house-price growth assumption behind Labour's CGT revenue. · interest.co.nz

  7. “I don't want it going heaps over or heaps under. For me, [it] is just making sure there is... steady and stable house prices.”

    7 October 2026 · Same interview, asked what Labour wants for house prices. · interest.co.nz

  8. “Tax cuts need to be allowed as the fiscal and economic environment allows for it.”

    7 October 2026 · Same interview, on whether income-tax changes are possible once in surplus. · interest.co.nz

Reversals or contradictions

None noted in our sources.

Profile

Role
Finance spokesperson
Electorate / list
Electorate MP (Mana); contesting the new Kenepuru electorate
Background
MP for Mana since 2020. Minister of Revenue, Internal Affairs and Pacific Peoples in the final year of the Labour government (2023). Now Labour's finance spokesperson.

Background source: Wikipedia

Key positions

  • Defends the CGT revenue assumptions (about 3% house-price growth) as 'conservative'.
  • Says income-tax cuts should come 'as the fiscal and economic environment allows'.
