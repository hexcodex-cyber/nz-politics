Chris Bishop

National: Attorney-General (since April 2026); Minister of Housing; Infrastructure; Transport; RMA Reform

What they've actually said

Direct quotes from 2025–26, newest first. Context lines are ours. Wording is as published in the linked source.

  1. “I will consider the reforms to have failed.”

    25 September 2026 · Newsroom interview: if, in five years, the new planning system has not made the fast-track regime unnecessary. · Newsroom

  2. “Today is a major milestone. After 35 years of the RMA, Parliament has passed legislation for a fundamentally different way of planning for New Zealand's future.”

    22 September 2026 · Press release as the Planning Bill and Natural Environment Bill (RMA replacement) passed third reading. · National Party

  3. “Passing these Bills is not the end of the job. The next task is making sure the new system works in practice.”

    22 September 2026 · Same release; the full new system is intended to operate by 2030. · National Party

  4. “I think what the Prime Minister was saying, which I would broadly agree with, is that the country has very difficult challenges ahead of it and we should spend our time focused on those challenges.”

    21 April 2026 · After voting in support of Luxon in the April caucus confidence vote; he described the caucus discussion as "good, honest and robust". · RNZ

  5. “Obviously I love being the campaign chair, and it's a role I enjoy doing, but it's fine - I've got plenty of things to do.”

    2 April 2026 · After losing the campaign-chair and Leader of the House roles in the April reshuffle while becoming Attorney-General. · RNZ

Profile

Role
Attorney-General (since April 2026); Minister of Housing; Infrastructure; Transport; RMA Reform
Electorate / list
MP for Hutt South; contesting Hutt South
Background
First entered Parliament as a list MP in 2014. Leader of the House until April 2026, and campaign chair until the April reshuffle, when he became Attorney-General. Led the RMA replacement and the first National Infrastructure Plan.

Background source: Wikipedia

Key positions

  • Planning reform: says the new system should make fast-track unnecessary within five years.
