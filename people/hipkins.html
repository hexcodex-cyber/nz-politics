Chris Hipkins

Labour: Leader of the Labour Party; Leader of the Opposition

What they've actually said

Direct quotes from 2025–26, newest first. Context lines are ours. Wording is as published in the linked source.

  1. “They've seen three years of this government. They don't want to see three more.”

    29 October 2026 · Responding to the RNZ–Reid Research poll (Labour 30.6%, National 24.8%), which he said showed 'real momentum' for change (reaction reported by RNZ on 29 Oct). · RNZ

  2. “This is just the National Party rehashing announcement after reannouncement after reannouncement. Half of this is stuff they promised to do at the last election and they haven't done it.”

    25 October 2026 · Wellington, responding to National's 100-day plan (RNZ live blog). · RNZ live blog

  3. “The cost of living is the number one issue in this year's election campaign.”

    24 October 2026 · Lower Hutt stand-up promoting Labour's cost of living action plan (RNZ live blog). · RNZ live blog

  4. “We can't go back on that decision, but we can figure out a way forward that protects New Zealand's aluminium industry, and we'll look to do that with a degree of urgency.”

    21 October 2026 · Visiting an aluminium plant in Hamilton, on the government's rejection of import safeguards (RNZ live blog). · RNZ live blog

  5. “I don't want to get into the territory that we've seen overseas, where a party that doesn't do as well as they hoped in the election starts to scream about the illegitimacy of the elections. That's not a territory that I want to get into.”

    18 October 2026 · TVNZ Q+A interview with Jack Tame, asked whether his criticism of the enrolment-law changes risked undermining the election's legitimacy. He said Labour would accept the result (1News). · 1News

  6. “You celebrate cutting jobs. I want to create them.”

    14 October 2026 · NZ Herald (NZME) leaders' debate with Chris Hipkins, 14 Oct evening, as reported in RNZ's analysis. Pointing to 48,000 more people unemployed than when Luxon took office. RNZ reports the two sentences either side of 'he chided Luxon'. · RNZ

  7. “Internally, I was in favour of campaigning on a CGT in 2020. We didn't do that, obviously. We've always been clear that we will only make changes to the tax system that we have a public mandate for.”

    11 October 2026 · Answering an audience question at a Birkenhead campaign meeting, the day Labour announced locally made school lunches from 2028 · 1News

  8. “#BREAKING: Labour will provide three free doctor's visits a year for everyone, delivered via a brand new Medicard and funded by a targeted tax on property profits, excluding the family home.”

    28 October 2025 · X post announcing Labour's flagship health/tax policy. · X post

Reversals or contradictions

  • In July 2023 he said: "under a government I lead there will be no wealth or capital gains tax after the election. End of story." In October 2025 Labour adopted a 28% CGT on property. After the 2023 loss he put tax options back on the table, and he says the CGT is narrow and targeted. [RNZ]
  • RNZ background on the 2025 decision and earlier ruling-out. [RNZ]

Profile

Role
Leader of the Labour Party; Leader of the Opposition
Electorate / list
MP for Remutaka (since 2008); contesting Remutaka
Background
Was Education Minister and Covid-19 Response Minister in the Ardern government, and Prime Minister from January to November 2023. Has led Labour since January 2023.

Background source: Wikipedia

Key positions

  • Election framed as being 'about the cost of living'.
  • A property-only capital gains tax to fund three free GP visits a year; a 10% student-loan write-off.
  • Says talks with Opportunity are 'conversations to be had after the election'.
