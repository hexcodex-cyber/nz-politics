What decides the government

2,888,890 party votes · 2:00pm Friday 27 November 2026 (NZDT) — official results

Electoral Commission official results, published 2pm Friday 27 November 2026, including special votes and a second count of ordinary votes. The Commission declares the official count by publishing a notice in the NZ Gazette. Candidates have three working days from this declaration to apply for a judicial recount (deadline Wednesday 2 December). The writ is due back on Thursday 3 December if nobody applies. Source: Electoral Commission.

Official count and talks

These are the official Electoral Commission results published at 2pm on Friday 27 November 2026, including special votes and a second count of ordinary votes. Compared with the election-night preliminary count, National lost two seats and the Greens gained one. The House has 130 seats (10 overhang) and 66 are needed to govern. The right bloc and the left-plus-Opportunity grouping are tied on 65. Candidates have three working days to apply for a judicial recount (deadline Wednesday 2 December).

  • 27 November 2026

    The Electoral Commission published the official results at 2pm on Friday 27 November. Labour 29.88% (863,238 party votes, 22 electorate and 14 list, 36 seats), National 28.35% (819,012, 40 electorate and 0 list, 40), Greens 14.06% (406,356, 3 and 14, 17), ACT 10.54% (304,521, 1 and 12, 13), NZ First 9.60% (277,565, 0 and 12, 12), Opportunity 6.06% (175,093, 0 and 7, 7), Te Pāti Māori 0.63% (18,310, 5 and 0, 5). Total party votes 2,888,890. The House has 130 seats, with a 10-seat overhang (six from National, four from Te Pāti Māori), and 66 are needed to govern. Compared with the preliminary count, National lost two seats and the Greens gained one; other party totals are unchanged. The right bloc (National, ACT and NZ First) has 65 seats and the left (Labour, Greens and Te Pāti Māori) 58; left plus Opportunity is also 65. [Electoral Commission]

  • 27 November 2026

    Three electorates flipped from the preliminary count. Labour's Kieran McAnulty took Wairarapa from National's Mike Butterick by 81 votes (Butterick led by 139 on election night). Labour's Georgie Dansey took Hamilton East from National's Ryan Hamilton by 173 votes (Hamilton led by 456). The Greens' Julie Anne Genter took Wellington Bays from Labour's Craig Renney by 1,610 votes (Renney led by 140); Renney is replaced by Labour's next list candidate. Watched seats that did not flip: Labour's Michael Wood held Mt Roskill, ahead of National's Carlos Cheung by 1,128 (was 262); Te Pāti Māori's Aperahama Edwards held Te Tai Tokerau, ahead of the Greens' Hūhana Lyndon by 583 (was 436); Labour's Ginny Andersen held Hutt South, ahead of National's Chris Bishop by 1,485 (was 889); National's Blair Cameron held Nelson, ahead of Labour's Rachel Boyack by 1,083. Other close official margins include National's Rima Nakhle in Takanini by 459 and National's Lloyd Budd in Whangārei by 685. Candidates have three working days from the official declaration to apply for a judicial recount (deadline Wednesday 2 December). RNZ said a recount in Wairarapa is likely. [Electoral Commission]

  • 27 November 2026

    RNZ reports that the official count has erased the coalition's majority, leaving Christopher Luxon reliant on Opportunity to break a deadlock and avoid a return to the polls. Opportunity is pivotal but, in RNZ's wording, short of being a true kingmaker, because Labour, the Greens, Te Pāti Māori and Opportunity together also have 65 seats, one short of a majority. RNZ said the most plausible path is for Opportunity to support the centre-right on key votes, or to abstain, in exchange for policy concessions; Qiulae Wong could also seek ministerial jobs or a four-party coalition. RNZ understands all four parties have had very preliminary staff discussions, mostly on the rules of engagement. Final turnout was 77.2% of enrolled voters (78.2% in 2023), and about 26,000 votes were disallowed. The government stays in caretaker mode until a new administration is sworn in. Parliament must meet by 14 January 2027. [RNZ]

  • 27 November 2026

    On Ryan Bridge TODAY, before the official count was released, ACT leader David Seymour said a four-party government "may well be necessary… unless there are four parties", that ACT had talked "to Opportunity, New Zealand First and National at leader and staff level… all pretty preliminary", and that "you can definitely do two. You can do one, but it's touch and go." [Ryan Bridge TODAY]

  • 9 November 2026

    RNZ analysis set out how a handful of close electorates or a small shift in the party vote could remove the right's one-seat majority, or leave Opportunity able to give either side 66 seats. National's party vote is currently worth 35 of the 120 standard seats, so it keeps all 42 electorate leads as overhang; Te Pāti Māori's party vote is worth one seat, with four more overhang. On a strictly proportional 120-seat basis the two blocs would be tied at 60. [RNZ]

  • 10 November 2026

    National's Christopher Luxon met Opportunity leader Qiulae Wong for an hour in his office on Monday night, after ruling her party out during the campaign. On Tuesday he called the conversation "very positive, very constructive". He said formal talks with ACT and NZ First would wait until the specials were counted, and, asked about the finance portfolio, said: "That'll be a National Party MP." He also said he had spoken briefly with David Seymour and Winston Peters to congratulate them. [RNZ]

  • 10 November 2026

    Wong met Labour's Chris Hipkins for about an hour on Tuesday. An Opportunity spokesperson said they talked generally about the campaign and a desire for more collaboration, and that negotiations would wait until the final count. Wong told 1News the party would first talk with National, as the largest party, and "will certainly speak to Labour" if those talks cannot move forward. A spokesperson said Opportunity had had no word from Winston Peters or David Seymour at that stage, had exchanged contact details after bumping into Te Pāti Māori members, and was due to meet Green co-leader Chlöe Swarbrick later on Tuesday evening. Whether that Green meeting took place has not been independently confirmed here. [RNZ]

  • 8 November 2026

    On Sunday, Swarbrick welcomed Opportunity into Parliament but said "it's time to pick a side" and that the party no longer got to "sit on the fence". The same morning on Q+A, NZ First deputy Shane Jones said the party would "just wait until what the specials reveal" and was happy it appeared the existing National–ACT–NZ First arrangement could be retained. National campaign chair Simeon Brown called the result "bittersweet" because of senior MPs likely to leave Parliament, and said a hung Parliament "would ultimately mean another election". [1News]

  • 10 November 2026

    National has no list seats on the preliminary count, so list-only MPs Nicola Willis, Paul Goldsmith and Gerry Brownlee are out unless that changes. Chris Bishop, also a list candidate, trails in Hutt South by 889 votes. On Monday night he posted that he had rung Ginny Andersen to concede, describing election night as "soul-destroying" and saying that although specials remain, they were unlikely to reverse the result. On Tuesday he told Morning Report the vote-splitting and overhang campaigns were "terribly confusing for voters". [RNZ]

  • 12 November 2026

    The Post reported informal discussions on the right ahead of the specials. Luxon said he had spoken "very briefly" with Seymour and Peters and that negotiations could begin "in earnest" after the special votes. Seymour told The Post ACT was "already in preliminary discussions so we can get as much of the negotiations done as possible". NZ First chief of staff Darroch Ball said: "We haven't had any official talks with anyone – pre-negotiation or otherwise." [The Post]

  • 16 November 2026

    On Breakfast, Seymour said he and Luxon had met in person for "chats about the chats" and had started talking about what a deal might look like; he had not spoken to Peters at that stage. He floated a single three-party coalition agreement rather than last term's separate agreements. 1News reported staff from ACT had also spoken with staff from Opportunity and from NZ First. [1News]

  • 19 November 2026

    The Electoral Commission said a data-entry error on election night had recorded zero Opportunity votes at an Arrowtown booth; it was corrected in the official count. Chief electoral officer Karl Le Quesne said preliminary results are "indicative" and may contain errors, that all ordinary votes had been counted a second time, and that the commission was "on track" to publish the final result on 27 November. The preliminary figures shown on this site are therefore subject to small changes. [RNZ]

  • 24 November 2026

    The Spinoff (opinion/analysis) reported that Opportunity leader Qiulae Wong has said Winston Peters' place as foreign minister is "untenable" and "We wouldn't want to be part of a government where he is still in that role"; Opportunity also has five bottom lines, including phasing out bottom trawling. It cited projections (The Overhang, The Post, electionhq.nz) that Opportunity is more likely than not to decide the next government; these are modelled estimates, not results. [The Spinoff]

  • 25 November 2026

    Peters arrived in Wellington for a function for outgoing Speaker Gerry Brownlee and declined to take questions on Michael Laws or on whether he would negotiate with Labour after the final results. The NZ Herald noted he has previously ruled Labour out and said he did not want to work with Opportunity. Newstalk ZB reported on 23 November that Luxon, Seymour and Peters were back in the Cabinet meeting room. [NZ Herald]

  • 26 November 2026

    The Spinoff reports the Electoral Commission will release the final results at 2pm Friday 27 November (subject to potential recounts). About 399,365 special votes (14.1% of the estimated total) were not in the election-night count, an estimated 96,947 of them overseas/dictation votes. Specials have historically exceeded early estimates. This site will update to official seats once published. [The Spinoff]

What matters now

On the official count the right bloc (National + ACT + NZ First) has 65 seats in a 130-seat House, one short of the 66 needed to govern. Opportunity's seven seats would take the right to 72, or leave a 65–65 hung parliament if it sided with the left (Labour + Greens + Te Pāti Māori + Opportunity also total 65). National still has no list MPs, so a recount that flipped one of its electorates would shrink both National and the House.
