General election

Saturday 7 November 2026

A plain-language catch-up on New Zealand's parties and their leading figures. It is built on what politicians have actually said in 2025–26, with every quote dated and linked to its source.

Key dates

  • Election day: Saturday 7 November 2026. Prime Minister Christopher Luxon announced the date on 21 January 2026. [RNZ]
  • Advance voting: Began on Monday 26 October 2026 (Labour Day), with party leaders among those voting on day one (RNZ, 26 Oct), and runs to Friday 6 November. Voting is in person (online voting only for overseas voters). 207 voting places will open on Labour Day (Monday 26 October), offering 1,437 hours of voting, and more will open closer to election day; hours vary, so check vote.nz first. On election day, Saturday 7 November, more than 2,300 voting places will be open from 9am to 7pm. EasyVote cards are not required (Chief Electoral Officer Karl Le Quesne, 22 October). Electoral Commission advice released on 18 October called the date 'challenging, but manageable', partly because voting opens on a public holiday. [RNZ]
  • Enrolment: Closed at midnight on Sunday 25 October 2026 (RNZ, 26 October). You cannot enrol or update details after that, and you cannot enrol at a voting place. Chief Electoral Officer Karl Le Quesne said every complete application received by the deadline will be processed in time, including forms arriving in the post on Tuesday 27 October; enrolment events were held around the country on the final weekend (RNZ, 23 October). On 25 October Le Quesne told TVNZ's Q+A it was 'fair' to say the earlier deadline would affect the number enrolled, but 'hard to say' how much; the Commission will analyse the impact after the election (RNZ, 1News). Labour said some people had struck problems with the online enrolment form; the Commission said it was not aware of any technical issues (RNZ live blog, 25 Oct). [RNZ]
  • Enrolment figures: Latest: as of Friday 23 October, with two days left, 92.4% of eligible people had enrolled, compared with 94.7% in 2023 (RNZ, 26 Oct), and about 70% of 18–24-year-olds, compared with about 77% on election day 2023 (1News, 25 Oct). These broadly match the unverified figures below. Final enrolment figures had not been published when we checked. Earlier: as at 21 October, 3,729,667 people were enrolled, 92.1% of eligible voters, below the 94.7% reached in 2023 but already more than the 3,688,292 enrolled at that election. 18–24-year-olds remain the least enrolled (302,475, or 68.6%). These are Electoral Commission figures released on 22 October (RNZ). (An earlier RNZ report on 22 October gave 91.2% and 65.5%; the Commission's newer release supersedes it.) A later Commission release, reported only by a secondary source we could read (The Gist, 24 Oct; the Commission's own site blocks our checks), gave 92.2% enrolled and 307,991 eligible people still unenrolled as at 23 October, with 18–24-year-olds on 69.9% (77.2% in 2023); treat these as unverified. Enrol online at vote.nz, by post or at an enrolment hub. Same-day enrolment during advance voting and on election day has been abolished. [RNZ]
  • Overseas voting: Opened on Wednesday 21 October 2026, along with telephone dictation voting for blind and some disabled voters. About 126,000 enrolled voters have told the Electoral Commission they live overseas (92,600 in 2023), 64% of them in Australia (Electoral Commission figures, RNZ live blog, 21 October). By 25 October Le Quesne said more than 132,000 New Zealanders overseas had enrolled, about two-thirds of them in Australia, and just over 20,000 had downloaded voting papers; the Commission sent 2.2 million email reminders, followed by texts (RNZ, 25 Oct). Some overseas voters reported that Adobe Reader's 'Fill & Sign' tool would not let them tick some parties' circles; the Commission said the voting paper is a standard PDF, not a fillable form, that voters can annotate manually, use another reader or print and fill it in by hand, and that a vote counts as long as the voter's intention is clear (1News, 24 Oct). [RNZ]
  • Recent debates: The third and final Luxon v Hipkins head-to-head (Stuff and The Post) was held on Tuesday 20 October. The NZ Herald 'Powerbrokers' debate, moderated by Ryan Bridge, was held on Wednesday 21 October with Marama Davidson (Greens), David Seymour (ACT), Winston Peters (NZ First) and Qiulae Wong (Opportunity). Te Pāti Māori did not take part after the death of co-leader Debbie Ngarewa-Packer's mother (RNZ live blog, 21 October). [RNZ]
  • Official results: The official count is due on Friday 27 November 2026. Luxon said formal coalition talks would probably wait for it, and neither he nor Hipkins would promise a deal before Christmas (RNZ, 19 October). [RNZ]
  • Parliament: The 54th Parliament was dissolved on 1 October 2026. Ministers stay in office until a new government is formed. [Wikipedia]
  • Four-year term referendum: This will not be held with the 2026 election. The government dropped the planned referendum on 18 February 2026 (secondary source: Wikipedia). [Wikipedia]

The story so far

The current government is the Sixth National Government, formed in November 2023. National leads it with two separate coalition agreements, one with ACT and one with NZ First. Winston Peters was Deputy Prime Minister until 31 May 2025, when David Seymour took over under the agreed rotation.

Seats at dissolution: National 49, ACT 11 and NZ First 8 (government total 68). Labour 34, Greens 15, Te Pāti Māori 4 and two independents (both former Te Pāti Māori MPs). That is 123 seats in total.

Polls in the final month show both major parties well below their 2023 results (64.9% together). The latest 1News–Verian poll (15–19 October, published 20 October) had National up four points to 32% and Labour down one to 27%, Labour's lowest in that series since the last election; Greens 16%, ACT 11%, NZ First 8%, Opportunity 7% and Te Pāti Māori 0.7%. On 1News' projections National, ACT and NZ First would have 60 seats, one short of a majority in a 120-seat Parliament, so the right could govern only with Opportunity, which Luxon has ruled out; the left's path depends on Te Pāti Māori winning enough electorates to create an overhang. Preferred PM: Luxon 20%, Hipkins 17%. The same day The Post/Freshwater Strategy poll had Labour narrowly ahead (28.7% v 27.6%) with Opportunity on 5.4%, just above the threshold. Hipkins called the Verian result an outlier; Peters called it 'crap'. Earlier, the 13 October Verian poll had the majors tied on 28%, and a leaked 6–10 October Anacta poll (Labour's own pollster) had Labour 29%, National 28%.

Leader ratings: in 1News Vote Compass (136,713 participants, 4–11 October, weighted, non-probability), no leader averaged 5 out of 10 for favourability. Hipkins was highest on 4.3, then Swarbrick 4.0, Wong 3.8, Peters 3.7 and Luxon 3.6; Seymour 3.4, Davidson 3.3, Ngarewa-Packer 2.1 and Waititi 1.9 (1News, 17 Oct).

Health: in the 20 October 1News–Verian poll 70% of voters said the public health system is in crisis and 28% said it faces challenges but is not in crisis (1News, 20 Oct). Health has become the second-biggest campaign issue after the cost of living, and dominated the final leaders' debate.

Cost of living and schools: in the same 15–19 October 1News–Verian poll (published 21 October), National and Labour were tied on 28% as the party voters most trust to tackle the cost of living, ahead of the Greens (10%), ACT (7%), NZ First (6%), Opportunity (3%) and Te Pāti Māori (1%); 17% named none or did not know. 70% wanted to keep the rule that primary schools teach an hour each of reading, writing and maths every day, and 24% wanted it removed (1News, 21 Oct).

Inflation: annual inflation eased to 3.7% in the September quarter, from 4.1% (Stats NZ, 22 October), and is expected to rise again once the fuel-price spike is captured. Nicola Willis called it encouraging; Labour's Willow-Jean Prime pointed to unemployment at an 11-year high and 30,000 more people on Jobseeker; David Seymour said that without fuel prices inflation was within the Reserve Bank's band (RNZ; RNZ live blog, 22 Oct).

Late deciders: in the final Post/Freshwater Strategy poll (9–19 October, 1458 voters, ±3%), only 14% said they always vote for the same party; 34% had decided in the past few months (12% in the past few weeks) and 15% were still undecided. Half planned to vote on election day and 34% during advance voting. 26% of NZ First supporters and 23% of Opportunity supporters had decided in the past few weeks. In Auckland (426 likely voters) National led Labour 37.7% to 26.4%, while Hipkins was preferred PM over Luxon 45.9% to 41.1% (The Post, 23 Oct; The Post, 22 Oct).

Tax on the wealthy: in 1News Vote Compass (186,475 participants, 4–21 October, weighted, non-probability), 62% said wealthier people should pay somewhat or much more tax, down from 67% in 2023 but a majority in every Vote Compass survey since 2014, including 53% of those earning over $100,000. By current party support: Greens 96%, Labour 88%, Opportunity 86%, NZ First 45%, National 30%, ACT 25% (1News, 24 Oct).

Big issues of 2026: the cost of living (made worse by a fuel-price spike linked to the 2026 Middle East conflict); tax (Labour's capital gains tax, the Greens' wealth taxes, National's 'no new taxes' pledge); two challenges to Luxon's leadership inside National (April and August); the law replacing the RMA, passed in September; Treaty and Waitangi Tribunal policy; immigration; and turmoil inside Te Pāti Māori.

How this was made

For each party in Parliament we profile the leader(s) plus four other senior figures, mainly the next places on the party's 2026 list (details on each party page). Quotes were collected only from sources we actually retrieved: news reports (RNZ, 1News, Newsroom, The Spinoff, Te Ao Māori News and others), party and ministerial press releases, and politicians' own X posts. If a quote could not be verified, it was left out. Where a person has fewer than four verified quotes, we say so. Full methodology and source list →
